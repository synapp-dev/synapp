3.  Reasonable, firm and realistic: the pricing band

The signed $30,000 for the content type is itself the strongest pricing signal: its delivery cost is only ~$10,550, so the client has already accepted roughly a 2.8× markup over cost on this work. Applying the same logic gives a defensible band:

Cost floor — the firm, conservative number: $229,394 for the additional scope ($251,931 whole program). Hard to argue down; it is market rate × honest hours.
Value basis — what the client's own agreement implies: ~$652,305 for the additional scope. Not aggressive; it just applies the markup they already signed for one item to the rest.
Recommendation: quote the additional scope on the cost-plus discipline basis as the floor, and hold the value figure as justified headroom in negotiation.
4.  Timeline

With one specialist per discipline working in parallel (~7 productive hours/day), calendar time is the longest single queue, not the sum of all hours:
Original agreement (content + bugs): ~6 weeks, as committed.
Full additional scope: backend is the critical path at ~24 weeks (823 hours). Frontend ~21 weeks. So the additional scope is a ~24-week (≈5.4-month) program with parallel help, or proportionally longer solo.
Practical implication: the additional scope cannot be absorbed into the original 6-week window. It is a separate phase with its own schedule and price.
